# Map geometry and roads

- Anything about where things stand, what a road does or what a volume covers is settled from the scene's own data before it is coded or claimed, and never from a plausibility sketch: the volumes' boxes, the use boxes, the locators and the cooked navmesh are all on disk. A two-dimensional straight-line check of the tarot filter in September 2026 missed everything that mattered (a corner clip, a volume two storeys up, turned boxes, a corridor that only the mesh knew), and every one of those cost a round of the maintainer's play.
- The geometry is three-dimensional and turned, always. A brush volume is its element box under the component's scale and yaw (the engine's rotation matrix: positive yaw takes local X towards world Y; scale first, then the turn, then the placement), or its collision hull when the cooked asset keeps one, which it mostly does not. `GetActorBounds`, what the mod reads in play, is the axis-aligned bounds of that turned box, up to half again as large as the box itself, so a test against bounds says "inside" where the box is not; eleven of the 26 tarot volumes are turned (37 degrees for Justice, 80 for the Lovers). Heights decide as much as the plan: the lodge of chapter 10 has three storeys 300 cm apart, and a road on the ground ran "through" a volume in the attic in two dimensions. Every road and every volume is judged with its height range and the capsule's, and a road is sampled as finely as the mod samples it (25 cm), every point, never its corners alone.
- A test is only as good as its match with the game's own: the game counts a character in a volume once its capsule touches it, so a road reveals a card where a point of it comes within the capsule's radius of the collision, and a stricter test (the whole capsule inside, asked for after a corner clip that had another cause) hid nothing thin: the Devil's volume is a strip 48 cm wide laid across a path. And the roads to judge by are all the roads a player walks, from where they stand and between the things listed, not the roads from one spot: the strip lay off every road from the scene's start and across the roads between the objects. Before a rule of thumb goes in, find the game's condition class in the dump and its history (`UGFConditionInVolume`, `UGFConditionEverInVolume`, `GFActionClearVolumeHistory`), and replay the whole exploration, every pair of targets, not the first leg.
- The asset is set against what the game logs before it is trusted. The element box can be stale (the Sun's tarot volume: 202 by 67 cm in the asset, 239 by 220 in play); a volume's pivot can stand metres from the box it fills (the Tower's brush sits 8 m along its own axis from the actor, which is what made the logged distance to its middle confirm the turn's sign); a size the mod logs, a distance to a middle, a road's length and its number of corners are the checks, and a residual of centimetres is the standard, not "close enough".
- `fetch_scene.py` is the way in: the persistent map `Maps/Acts/<act>/<scene>/<map>/<map>.umap` with its `.uexp` holds the navmesh; the sub-levels under `gameplay/` (`Gameplay/`, or `Levels/` for the Hackett house) hold the triggers, interactions, locators, props and cameras. UAssetGUI's JSON wraps a struct value as a one-element list of property data, keeps `FActorReference` values as base64 raw structs (int32, FString, 2 bytes), rotators as Pitch/Yaw/Roll, and an actor's location on its root component. Use boxes are the `BoxComponent` of the use location, default extents 85 by 85 by 75 from `DefaultGame.ini`.
- The navmesh, as `navmesh.py` reads it: tiles found by the magic `VAND`, a standard 100-byte dtMeshHeader plus four ints, then vertices and 32-byte polygons; a tile is 1000 cm, and one position can hold several layers (the ground and the walkable top of something), each a tile of its own. Recast keeps (x, y, z) = (-X, Z, -Y) of the game. The links are rebuilt exactly as `dtNavMesh::addTile` rebuilds them: inner neighbours by index, portals by `findConnectingPolys` (matched by side, by the line they lie on, and by slabs overlapping along it within the walkable climb, in Recast's own coordinates), each link keeping the overlapping part of the edge as its portal. A home-made matching by edge line, span and midpoint height left the mesh in islands and bent roads along tile borders, 3 to 16 percent long. The road is `findPath` (A* with node positions at the portal midpoints, nodes keyed by polygon and the tile side crossed into it, the heuristic scaled by 0.999) and then `findStraightPath`; its restart `i = apexIndex; continue;` sits in a `for` loop, so the scan resumes one portal later, and a `while` loop must say so or it spins for ever. Validated against the mod's log: 60-odd roads in seven scenes within 0.1 to 4 percent, most with the same corners; the short roads to use locations come out long because the mod aims at a spot of the use box it settles later. The game's paths carry the pawn's own location as their first point and one more point where a road crosses between navigation areas.
- The mesh in play is not always the cooked one: the game runs `RuntimeGeneration=DynamicModifiersOnly`, its own `NavigationSystemSMG`, `ANavigationBlocker` volumes that doors spawn and flows switch, and `GFActionSetMainNavMesh` naming the scene's map. In the woodland date the ground at the base of the trail cameras is an island of its own layer in the tiles, and the game's road ended on it, complete; and the mod, testing the volume's own collision along the road the game gave it, found the Tower's volume clear of every road while every cooked-mesh road runs through its middle. A prediction is presented as a prediction, with what remains untestable offline said in the same breath, and the log of a session decides.
- The character is placed from the log, not assumed: the mod logs the distance to every listed place and use location at a hand-over, and a grid search over the scene's heights with a refinement puts the character within centimetres (`roadvalidate.py`); a locator from the flow is the fallback. The pawn's location the game reports is the actor's, which the fit puts well above the floor, so heights are taken from the mesh and the actors, never from that point.
- Working habits that this cost: read the actual source (Detour's is public) rather than recalling it; a fact about the game (a class, a property, a flag) comes from the class dump or the asset, not from its name; search the whole data set, not the part where the answer is expected (the Strength's cutscene collect sat in a scene's main flow while the exploration flows were the only ones searched, and an exploration flow can be dead content that no transition enters); and keep the research scripts that are specific to one feature in `research\`, git-ignored, building on these modules.
