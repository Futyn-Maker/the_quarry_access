# Menus, pause menu, HUD, subtitles, credits, screens, tarot

- The menu framework is Blueprint (`MenuButton_C`, `MenuCarousel_C`, `TextSlider_C`, `KeyRemap_C`, `NestedContentMenu_C`, popups built on `PopupScreenBaseWidget_C`); the native `USMGUIButton*` classes are unused.
- Row labels by type, in this order: sliders and carousels read their `TitleText` block (the rendered order puts the value first); `MenuButton_C` resolves its `LocalisedText` locale key (a deep walk finds the highlight decoration twice); `KeyRemap_C` and `TutorialButton_C` have no key and nest deeper, so only they get the deep walk.
- Buttons, titles and prompts are added at runtime: find them among live instances and walk up through `Slot->Parent` (`obj::IsDescendantOf`) rather than down from the screen. Prompt widgets keep a hidden placeholder text on their unused side. A screen's description line comes from `UMenuBarBaseSMG026::GetUnlocalisedMenuContext()`; `MenuContext.Text` stays a placeholder forever.
- Text sources beyond `Text`: a localised block (`SMGLocalisedTextBlock`, `LocalisedTextBlockQuarry`) often holds only its `LocaleString` key; a bound property is fed by its companion delegate (`TextDelegate`, `HintTextDelegate`), which `obj::BoundText` calls; an `EditableTextBox` holds what was typed only behind `GetText()`, its `Text` being the editor's placeholder. `ReadTextLeaf` and `obj::TextOf` cover all three, so every walk sees the drawn text.
- A screen names its own title in a `Title` property (a `MenuTitle_C` or a text block); it wins over a search of live `MenuTitle_C`, since screens head their lists with more of them (the Wolf Pack lobby). A screen with none of the known containers gets its body from its own tree minus the controls, prompts, bottom bar, pause tabs, the headline of a list and a selector's `ModeDescription_txt`; widgets appearing there are excluded whether or not they are drawn this moment, since a fading bar still holds its placeholder.
- The big headline of a list tab (`CollectablesTitle_C`, `PathChosenTitle_C`) is not the screen's title but the name of the item the selection is on, so it is read with that item and nowhere else. Its subtitle is where the clue was found.
- A list of clues, evidence, tarot cards, places or paths shows the item it is on in the panel beside it, all at once, so that panel is the selection's description: where it was found, then its lines. A line is a `CollectablesInfoButton_C`, and the game hides one whose story it has not reached with `NOT IsCollectableUnlocked OR IsCollectableNew` (and behind a picture from the scene, its `VideoBox` drawn), leaving the words in place at no opacity and showing the mark in `StatusText` instead. The same rule reads a line wherever it is read: in the panel beside the list, and when the player steps into the panel and the focus is on the line itself.
- A control the selection moves to is read whatever it says, even the same words as the one before: the locked rows of a list all show the placeholder, and a check that skipped a description equal to the last one read left every second locked row silent as the player moved (the log of 2026-09-22 14:37, clues and paths). What one control says twice in quick succession is deduped by `speech::Focus` within `[Speech] FocusDedupeMs`; the feature keeps no text of its own for it.
- Sections of a list are `CollectablesGroup_C`; their name is read when the selection moves into another one, either direction. A selection that belongs to no section is beside the list, not in it, and leaves the section it was in standing.
- A path opened from the paths tab shows a film strip of the moments reached (`PathChosenCarouselBase_C`, `CurrentButton`/`ButtonIndex`/`ButtonCount`), which keeps the focus on itself while the arrows move it. The moments are pictures of their scenes and carry no words: the path's name is `CarouselTitle` (a `MenuTitle_C`) and the line about the moment the strip stands on is the carousel's own `TitleText`. It is not a setting and is not announced as one.
- `WidgetSwitcher` draws only its active page (`ActiveWidgetIndex` into `Slots`), and the inactive pages keep their visibility flags: `obj::IsWidgetShown` and the text walk honour it. A screen that turns a page of its own is read again like a section; pages turned while it is being read belong to that reading, and a rereading with nothing new says nothing. The Wolf Pack host fills its lobby page (description, player count) only after turning to it, about a second after `Show`.
- The 2K account screens (`Maps/Frontend/Menus/DNA`, `SA01`–`SU03`) are ordinary `MenuBaseWidget_C` screens with `Dna*Button_C` buttons and `SMGEditableTextBox` fields named by their bound hint. The game walks them, and any screen with a text field, with Tab (`help.form`): the arrows are game actions that only a menu bar consumes, and Backspace edits the field. A text field has no highlight, so the focus watcher asks the few live ones `HasKeyboardFocus`. A failed sign-in rewrites the same screen, so a screen's `Title`/`SubTitle`/`Body`/`BodyText` blocks are watched.
- A prompt with `CollapseText` shows its key alone; `MenuPromptWidget_C`'s default `Text` is the placeholder "test". A `MenuCarousel_New_C` that shows its own `PromptNext`/`PromptPrev` is turned by `[`/`]` wherever the focus is (the Wolf Pack mode: Lone Wolf, Wolf Pack; Alpha Wolf is disabled in `WolfPackModeSetting`): it is part of the screen's body and read when it turns. At either end of its list one of the two prompts is hidden.
- Focus can land on a part inside a control (`PopupButtonPanel_C` inside `BasicPopupButton_C`); `ui::Interactable` prefers the ancestor of a specific kind.
- There is no state the whole game shares for a control that cannot be used: `UIInteractableWidgetBaseSMG026` and `UIInteractableWidgetSMG026_C` have none, `IsButtonInteractable()` on the collectables base returns true whatever the state, and `SetIsEnabled` is used by a handful of widgets on their inner button. Each family keeps its own flag — `MenuButton_C` `bIsDisabled` (with `SetGreyed`), `PauseTabWidget_C` `IsDisabled`, the DNA buttons `bStartDisabled`, the collectables family `bIsCollectableUnlocked` — and `ui::Describe` asks all of them for one "unavailable".
- How the game tells the player differs within one family, so what the mod adds differs with it: a clue, a piece of evidence or a tarot card that is still locked has its name replaced by the placeholder in `HiddenNameString` ("????"), while a tutorial video keeps its name and is only drawn dim (`UpdateNameText` branches on the collectable being valid, `UpdateStateVisuals` colours it by `IsCollectableUnlocked`). The state is said where the name does not already stand for it, which is the placeholder itself or a label with no letter or digit in it.
- The pause menu is several sibling top-level widgets: the tab bar `PauseTabSystemWidget_C`, one content widget per tab and `CharacterCarousel_C`. Read only the selected tab's content, since tabs visited earlier stay loaded and shown for a while, and require `IsWidgetShown`. The character tab shows only a 3D model: nothing beyond the tab, the objective and the prompts is read there.
- The game builds the whole menu anew at every opening and leaves the last one alive until it is collected, so nothing about it survives an opening: the bar is taken only while it is drawn (`ui::PauseTabSystem`), and the menu's identity as one screen is the content of the tab on display (`ui::PauseAnchor`), which the bar and the carousel come and go around. A bar kept because it was still live left the menu with no tabs to report, for about a minute, at every opening after the first.
- The menu is up when the game calls one of its drawn tab screens the current one (`ui::PauseMenuState`, `IsCurrentScreen` up the chain from the tab). The bar's `bIsActive` is `NOT IsFocusSuspended()`: it drops whenever a popup or a path's film strip takes the focus, and stays raised long after the menu is gone, so it says nothing about the menu being up.
- The bar reports a new tab before the game draws that tab's content, so a reading asked for then is anchored on the tab being left; it follows the menu to whatever it ends up showing. A tab with nothing to select never moves the focus into itself, so on the character tab nothing else corrects that.
- The pause tabs do not announce themselves through `Show`: their content is shown before the menu around it is up, and the pause feature reads the menu as one screen once it is, heading it with `ui::PauseTabLine()` whoever asked for the reading.
- Answering the pause menu's quit popup is a game event: `PauseScreenBaseSMG026:OnQuitToFEPopupEnded(PopupIndexSelected)`, with `Popup_PauseMenuQuitToFE` holding Yes (confirm) at 0 and No (cancel) at 1. Confirming hands the character and its HUD prompts back for a few seconds while the game unloads, and none of that world is read (`features::LeavingTheGame`); cancelling leaves the menu standing with the focus nowhere, so the pause feature reads it again itself.
- The screen classes answer for themselves: `USMGUIWidget` has `IsCurrentScreen`, `GetUITransition`, `GetCurrentUITransition`, `CanTransition`, `LastFocusedWidget`; `USMGUIUserWidgetBase` under it has `IsScreenGroupTransitioningTo/FromNothing`, `DoesScreenGroupHaveFocus` and `IsFocusSuspended`. The transition answers belong to a whole screen group, so a popup closing over a screen makes that screen look as though it were leaving too.
- Couch co-op shares the eight characters out between players, and the game says who has whom only by where a name chip sits. Each chip (`CouchCo-opCharacterName_C`) and each carousel item (`CharacterCarouselItem_C`) holds the `CharacterInformationQuarry` it stands for, so the owner of the character on the carousel is the player button (`CouchCo-opPlayerButton_C`, its own name in `ItemScanlineText`) holding a chip for the same object — found by identity, never by matching the names on screen. Giving a character away and taking them back move no focus and change no card, so the owner of the character being shown is polled and a change of hands is said on its own.
- The character on a carousel is named by the title beside it on one screen (`CollectablesTitle_C` in the carousel's `Title`) and by the card itself on another, so anything added to that reading goes after whatever came first rather than at a fixed place in either.
- `CharacterInformationQuarry` and its base carry a name, name variants, bios and a subtitle colour, and no gender, so mod strings about a character are worded to need no agreement ("no player", "игрок не назначен").
- Couch co-op starts only once every character has a player and says so nowhere on the screen, so the mod says it once on arrival (`couch.hint`); its warning that players were left out (`CouchCo-opNoCharactersAssigned_C`) names them between `BodyText_pt1` and `BodyText_pt2`, one `CouchCo-opQuickStartPlayer_C` row apiece.
- Subtitles: `ActionHUDSubtitle.SubtitleWidgetInstance` (`SubtitleWidgetQuarry_C`) holds the lines in `ForegroundLines`, polled every frame; a line absent from the two previous polls is new. The native `SetCurrentText` is not hooked. `GetSubtitleSetting()`: 0 off, 1 on, 2 closed captions, 3 minimal. The last line stays available to F4 until the main menu shows (`MenuBaseWidget_C:Show` on `MainMenuWidget_C`), pauses included.
- Wolf Pack voting lives in the ordinary choice widgets: `PackName_txt`, `InfoText`, `NotificationText`, `VoteTotal`, and on the two-way container `LeftPlayerList`/`RightPlayerList` with `LeftPercent_txt`/`RightPercent_txt`; each option carries `VoteInfo`. The vote goes on after the player's own vote, so these lines are polled for finished choices too. They stay hidden in single player.
- HUD elements are read from a whitelist (`kReadableHuds` in `Hud.cpp`) once their text has been stable for two polls. The epilogue's fate cards are `AftermathText_C` under `ActionHUDAftermathTextSMG026.AftermathTextInstance`.
- Credits: the opening names come one by one (`IntroCredit_C`). The end rolls move faster than speech, so only "Credits" and the section titles are said by themselves (the first line of a song block); F6 reads the rows on screen with their roles, from each line's `Credit` struct.
- Screens: some text is a picture on a 3D monitor (textures under `Content/Flipbooks`, set by a cinematic's material track or a prop's material). `mod/screens.ini` maps each material to its English transcription, verbatim, since the game shows it in English in every language; the feature watches known meshes once a scene's marker material is loaded. A search for unread text must cover pictures, material tracks and Bink films, not only widgets.
- Tarot visions are Bink films (`Movies/Tarot/<Card>/*.bk2`, 34 of them: a card plus a variant). Every `BinkMediaPlayer` is found by class and polled; when a tarot URL starts playing, `tarot.<film file name>` is read from `mod/lang/tarot/`.
- A film first plays in the reading after the chapter its card lies in, and nowhere earlier: each `act<N>fortune_GameFlow` imports only that act's films, the reading shows one of them, and the pause menu replays it afterwards. So a description uses only what the game has given the player by the end of that chapter, and the film's own picture for the rest. Settled on 2026-09-23 from the cinematics' subtitle lines and the locale table: the creature is first shown plainly in the chapter 4 treehouse (the developers' name for that shot is `werewolfreveal`), the objectives call it "the animal" in chapter 5 and "the creature" in chapter 6, the word werewolf is first spoken by Max in Laura's chapter 7 flashback and explained to the counselors by Laura at the end of that chapter, and the first objective to say it is chapter 8's. Hence the cards up to chapter 5 (Temperance, the Star, the Tower, the Devil) say "a creature", and "werewolf" begins with the chapter 8 cards. Names go the same way: the cop is "the police officer" until chapter 7 names Travis (the objectives say "the cop" until then), Bobby is named from the chapter 5 radio call on (the chapter 4 Strength says "the hunter"), and Jed and Constance are first named in chapter 9, so the chapter 8 Emperor and Empress say "the old hunter" and "an old woman". What a syringe holds and where a sudden strength comes from is not on the screen and is not said.
- Tutorial overlays hold their own widget instances outside the HUD, so their `Construct` hooks are the primary detection there.
- Couch co-op and Wolf Pack widgets exist, hidden, in single player too: never strip or special-case them.
